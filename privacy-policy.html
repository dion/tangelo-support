Tangelo Privacy Policy

Effective 18 August 2026

The short version

Tangelo has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no servers of its own. Nothing you do in the app is reported to its developer, because there is nowhere for it to be reported to. Your library, your settings and your listening history stay on your device.

The app does make network requests, but only ones you asked for: to services you configured yourself, or to public catalogues it queries on your behalf to find a piece of artwork or a set of lyrics. Those are described in detail below.

What is stored on your device

  • Your music library database — songs, albums, artists, playlists, artwork and any lyrics that have been fetched or read from your files.
  • Your settings and preferences.
  • Your play history, and the queue of pending scrobbles if you have connected a scrobbling service.
  • Credentials: passwords and tokens for Subsonic or Jellyfin servers, a Last.fm session key, a ListenBrainz token. These are held exclusively in the iOS Keychain. They never leave your device except to authenticate directly against the one service each belongs to.

All of this lives inside the app's own sandboxed container. Nothing is backed up to a developer-run service. If you back up your device to iCloud or a computer, Apple's or your computer's backup terms govern that copy, not this policy.

Your media library

With your permission, Tangelo reads your Media & Apple Music library so those songs appear alongside your local files. That access is read-only and stays on the device: the app builds its own index and does not transmit your library anywhere.

Playback of Apple Music content happens through Apple's own system media service, and purchases happen through the App Store. Apple's privacy policy governs both. Tangelo has no visibility into your Apple Music account or your purchase details — for an in-app purchase it can see only whether an unlock is owned, which it reads back from the App Store on your device.

Requests the app makes for you

Each of these sends only what the request itself needs, and keeps the answer in the app's local cache. None of it passes through any server operated by Tangelo's developer, and none of it is retained by the developer, because the developer never receives it.

Subsonic and Jellyfin servers

If you add a server, the app connects directly to the address you entered, using the credentials you entered, to browse and stream your music. Where the server is, who runs it, and what it logs are matters between you and that server.

Internet radio

Station search and browsing query the public radio-browser.info directory, which receives your search terms. Playing a station then connects your device directly to that station's stream, so the station's own host sees the connection.

Podcasts

Podcast feeds and episodes are fetched directly from the hosts of the feeds you add. Those hosts see the request in the same way any podcast app's request would be seen.

Lyrics

When you open the lyrics view for a song that has no lyrics embedded in the file and none already cached, the app asks lrclib.net for them, sending the track title, artist, album and duration so the right lyrics can be matched. If nothing is found, that result is cached too, so the same song is not asked about twice.

Album artwork

When an album has no artwork embedded in its files and none cached, the app looks it up by album and artist name at MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive, and at Apple's public iTunes Search API. Only those names are sent, and the image is stored locally once found.

Scrobbling

Scrobbling is off unless you turn it on and connect an account. If you do, the app sends what you played — track, artist, album, and the time you played it — directly to your own account at Last.fm or ListenBrainz, using the credentials you authorised. Nothing is sent to anyone else, and the developer never sees it. What those services do with your listening history is governed by their own privacy policies.

Children

Tangelo does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. It has no accounts, no messaging and no user-generated content.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your device: the library database, your settings, your play history, cached artwork and lyrics, and every credential it held in the Keychain. There is no developer-side copy to request or delete, because none was ever made.

Scrobbles you already sent are held by the service that received them. To remove those, use that service's own tools — Last.fm and ListenBrainz each let you delete listens from your account. Disconnecting scrobbling in Tangelo stops new ones and discards the local credentials, but cannot reach back into a third-party account.

Changes to this policy

If the app gains a new network destination or a new category of stored data, this page is updated before that version ships, and the effective date above changes with it.
